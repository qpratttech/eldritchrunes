# Eldritch Runes 2E

## Core System Rules

Venture beyond the last watchfires into a world of forgotten powers, haunted wilderness, and ancient ruins. Steel can keep you alive, but nerve, knowledge, and a little luck may carry you farther. Every battle leaves its mark, and even victory can demand time to recover.

Eldritch Runes uses percentile checks, a small pool of Wounds, and flexible actions. Build an adventurer from a species, background, class, and specialization, then develop their talents through play.

### Using This Book

Start with Playing the Game to learn checks, saving throws, Luck, and critical results. Creating a Character walks you through your first adventurer; Species, Creation Features, and Backgrounds provide your starting choices. Advancement and Classes explains how your character develops, with Specializations and the feature chapters describing the abilities you gain.

During play, refer to Combat for actions and movement, Damage, Defeat, and Recovery for injury and healing, and Conditions for ongoing effects. Equipment, Talents, and Tactics describe the tools and training available to adventurers. Magic and the Spellbook explain spellcasting, while Companions and Summoned Creatures covers the allies you command.

Rules use **character level** for your total levels across all classes and **class level** for levels in one class. A specific rule takes precedence over a general rule when their instructions differ. Prerequisites must be met when you select an option.

## 1 Playing the Game

The Game Master (GM) describes the world and portrays its inhabitants. Players describe what their adventurers attempt. When the outcome is uncertain and failure matters, the GM calls for a check. Ordinary tasks within a character's capabilities succeed without a roll.

You need percentile dice and six-sided dice. Read percentile results as 01–100; two zeroes mean 100 For 1d3, roll 1d6: 1–2 counts as 1, 3–4 as 2, and 5–6 as 3 Distances are given in feet; a square on a battle map normally represents 5 feet.

### The Core Check

**Attribute Step  Skill Rank  Tier Modifiers  Percentile Tier.**

Find the tier below and roll equal to or below its success percentage. A skill you have not trained has rank 0 Attributes and skills are numbers added together; neither is itself a percentage bonus.


| Tier | Success | Tier | Success   |
| ---- | ------- | ---- | --------- |
| 0    | 1%      | 10   | 59%       |
| 1    | 5%      | 11   | 65%       |
| 2    | 11%     | 12   | 71%       |
| 3    | 17%     | 13   | 77%       |
| 4    | 23%     | 14   | 83%       |
| 5    | 29%     | 15   | 89%       |
| 6    | 35%     | 16   | 95%       |
| 7    | 41%     | 17   | 99%       |
| 8    | 47%     | 18   | Automatic |
| 9    | 53%     |      |           |


 

A final tier of −1 or lower is impossible; a tier of 18 or higher succeeds automatically. Neither requires a roll. An automatic success is an ordinary success, not an automatic critical.

**Example:** Strength 8 and Melee rank 2 give tier 10, a 59% chance. Enhancement raises the check to tier 11, or 65%. One level of Suffering reduces that enhanced check to tier 10 again.

### Attributes and Skills

Agility measures coordination and speed; Intellect covers learning and perception; Resolve measures inner conviction; Social reflects presence and influence; Strength measures physical force; Vitality reflects health and endurance.


| Attribute | Associated skills                                                                                                            |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Agility   | Reflexes, Riding, Stealth, Ranged                                                                                            |
| Intellect | Awareness, Comprehension, Craft, Eldritch Lore, History, Medicine, Mythos, Nature Lore, Research, Sailing, Survival, Sorcery |
| Resolve   | Willpower, Faith                                                                                                             |
| Social    | Coercion, Deception, Diplomacy, Performance                                                                                  |
| Strength  | Athletics, Melee, Unarmed                                                                                                    |
| Vitality  | Endurance, Shamanism                                                                                                         |


 

**Attack skills** are Melee, Ranged, Unarmed, Sorcery, Faith, and Shamanism. All other skills are nonattack skills, even when used as saving throws. Faith and Shamanism remain attack skills when used outside combat.

Athletics covers climbing, swimming, lifting, and grappling. Reflexes covers quick reactions, balance, and delicate manual work, including locks and traps. Comprehension interprets languages, patterns, and unfamiliar concepts. Eldritch Lore concerns supernatural forces and alien phenomena; Mythos concerns religious traditions, sacred beings, and legends. Craft creates and repairs objects, with suitable tools and materials. Other skills apply as their names suggest; the GM selects the skill that best fits the approach.

### Difficulty and Circumstances


| Modifier             | Tier adjustment |
| -------------------- | --------------- |
| Enhanced             | 1               |
| Greater Enhancement  | 2               |
| Diminished           | −1              |
| Greater Diminishment | −2              |


 

An easy task grants Enhancement; an extremely easy task grants Greater Enhancement. A difficult task imposes Diminishment; an exceptionally difficult task imposes Greater Diminishment.

Count each applicable Enhancement as 1 and each Diminishment as −1; Greater effects count as two. Cancel opposing modifiers, then limit their combined adjustment to 2 or −2. Multiple copies of the same named effect do not stack. Suffering and Broken apply afterward and can take the total penalty below −2. A modifier affecting attacks does not automatically affect saving throws.

### Saving Throws and Unopposed Checks

A saving throw is a skill check made to resist harm. Endurance, Reflexes, and Willpower are the most common, but a rule can call for any skill. Apply the usual attribute, skill rank, and relevant modifiers. Saves can be made while unable to act unless an effect explicitly forbids them.

There are no contested rolls. Resolve an attempt with one character's check or the target's saving throw, as the rule directs—not both. For a grapple, spend a major action and make Athletics against a creature within 5 feet that you can physically restrain. Success makes it Held. The target can escape with its normal condition save. The GM may apply difficulty for a marked difference in size or leverage.

### Luck

After a percentile roll is revealed, but before its effects are resolved, spend 1 Luck to reverse its tens and ones digits. You may alter your own roll or another creature's roll that your character can perceive. A roll can be reversed only once. For example, 82 becomes 28 and 10 becomes 01 A roll of 100 cannot be reversed, and reversal cannot produce 100 Luck cannot affect a check that required no roll. Restore Luck to its maximum after a Full Rest.

### Critical Results

Your starting critical range is **01–03, inclusive**. A direct attack is critical only if the final roll both succeeds and falls within that range. A bonus of 10% to critical chance extends the range by ten percentage points: 01–03 becomes 01–13. Add different bonuses; the range cannot exceed 01–99, and a failed check never becomes a critical.

A critical deals 1 wound, or 2 wounds for a two-handed melee attack. Saving-throw attacks, including area attacks, cannot critically hit. On a successful nonattack check within your critical range, the GM may grant a modest benefit appropriate to the task, such as completing it faster or discovering an additional detail; this does not override explicit rules.

## 2 Creating a Character

Begin at character level 1 with the following characteristics.


| Characteristic                              | Default                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| All six attributes                          | 6 each, corresponding to tier 6 or 35% before skill ranks |
| Wounds                                      | 3                                                         |
| Stamina                                     | 1                                                         |
| Initiative Bonus                            | 0                                                         |
| Crit Range                                  | 01–03                                                     |
| Luck                                        | 1                                                         |
| Movement                                    | 20 feet per major action; 10 feet per minor action        |
| Sorcerous Power, Potential, Soul, and Focus | 0 each                                                    |


 

1. **Choose a species.** Gain its two Primary and two Secondary Features.
2. **Choose your variance.** Select two additional Primary Features and one additional Secondary Feature. Apply choices in an order that meets their prerequisites. These traits can represent individual aptitude, mixed ancestry, or unusual heritage.
3. **Choose a background.** Increase each of its two attributes by 1 step. Gain rank 1 in each of its three skills, its background feature, and its equipment.
4. **Choose a class.** Gain its starting role bonuses and class-level-1 feature.
5. **Assign starting skills.** Choose two different skills from your class's primary list and gain 2 ranks in each. Choose three other different skills from the complete skill list and gain 1 rank in each. Background ranks stack with these gains. These starting skill allocations are gained only once.
6. **Choose a specialization.** You may choose one specialization, regardless of class. Its features arrive at character levels 1, 3, 6, and 9 Choosing none grants no replacement benefit.
7. **Finish your equipment and resources.** Record maximum Wounds, Stamina, Luck, Sorcerous Power, Sorcerous Soul, Sorcerous Potential, and Focus. Start with all expendable resources full.

Attributes cannot exceed step 10 through character creation or advancement. Skill ranks and situational modifiers can still raise a check above tier 10 If a fixed creation feature would exceed the attribute cap, assign the excess step to another attribute below 10

All characters receive a backpack, sack, three torches, three rations, background clothing, a waterskin, and 10 gold, plus their background equipment. Purchase weapons and armor from this money; classes do not grant additional free equipment. An Adept can fight effectively unarmed. A feature that grants spells also supplies the capacity needed for those spells, as described in Magic.

## 3 Species

Your species describes inherited traits, not your character's beliefs or profession. The descriptions below suggest a place in the world without prescribing a personality. Each listed feature is defined in Creation Features.

### Drakith

Scaled and formidable, drakith carry an elemental inheritance in their blood. Their natural weapons make even an apparently unarmed drakith a dangerous opponent.

**Primary Features:** Strong, Tough.

**Secondary Features:** Bite/Claws, Elemental Resistance.

### Dwarf

Dwarves endure through stubborn conviction and practiced craft. Generations spent in stone halls and deep workings have sharpened their sight and steadied their will.

**Primary Features:** Tough, Strong Willed.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Skilled.

### Eldretchin

Eldretchin bear the touch of powers beyond the familiar world. Their unusual gifts often arrive alongside a vulnerability that betrays their otherworldly ancestry.

**Primary Features:** Attribute Focus, Talented.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Accursed.

### Elf

Elves combine graceful movement with memories and traditions reaching deep into the past. Their affinity for hidden lore can guide them toward scholarship, sorcery, or exploration.

**Primary Features:** Agile, Wise.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Ancient Knowledge.

### Gnome

Gnomes possess resilient bodies and inquisitive minds. They carry fragments of ancient knowledge into workshops, secluded communities, and far-ranging adventures.

**Primary Features:** Tough, Wise.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Ancient Knowledge.

### Halfling

Halflings often survive through quick feet, an easy manner, and remarkable good fortune. Their unassuming presence can conceal a daring traveler or a patient infiltrator.

**Primary Features:** Agile, Likable.

**Secondary Features:** Lucky, Stealthy.

### Halfogre

Halfogres possess imposing frames and the strength to make full use of them. Whether gentle or fierce, they are difficult to overlook and harder to stop.

**Primary Features:** Strong, Tough.

**Secondary Features:** Hulking, Brute.

### Halforc

Halforcs unite considerable strength with adaptable talents. Their inherited ferocity becomes a powerful tool when guided by training and purpose.

**Primary Features:** Strong, Talented.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Fury.

### Human

Humans find many paths to distinction. Adaptable skills and individual ambition carry them into unfamiliar lands, rival traditions, and unlikely alliances.

**Primary Features:** Attribute Focus, Talented.

**Secondary Features:** Skilled, Versatile.

### Lizardman

Lizardmen move with a hunter’s economy and withstand harsh conditions. Scales, keen night sight, and natural weapons suit a life close to the wilderness.

**Primary Features:** Agile, Tough.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Bite/Claws.

### Minotaur

Minotaurs bring formidable strength and a towering presence to any company. Their horns are both a sign of their heritage and a weapon that cannot easily be taken away.

**Primary Features:** Strong, Tough.

**Secondary Features:** Hulking, Horns.

### Orc

Orcs are physically powerful and resilient. Their ability to summon fierce bursts of aggression serves hunters, guardians, soldiers, and wanderers alike.

**Primary Features:** Strong, Tough.

**Secondary Features:** Nightvision, Fury.

## 6 Combat



### Initiative and Rounds

A round represents about 6 seconds. At the start of combat, roll 2d6 and add your Initiative Bonus. Act from highest total to lowest and keep that order. Ties go to the higher Initiative Bonus; if still tied, roll 1d6 each, rerolling further ties. For features comparing initiative, use the totals, not the tie-break order.

### Your Turn

Each turn gives you **one major action, two minor actions, and one free action**. You may take them in any order. You cannot exchange a major action for additional minor actions. Unused actions expire at the end of your turn unless you reserve minor actions for Responses.


| Action | Common Uses                                                                                                                              |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Major  | Attack; cast a spell; Dodge; Hinder; grapple; make a major move; attempt a demanding skill task                                          |
| Minor  | Make a minor move; stand; draw or stow an item; drink a potion; open a door; command a companion; activate an eligible feature or tactic |
| Free   | Brief speech, a signal, or dropping an object                                                                                            |


 

Brief speech does not compel a response or grant a Social check for free. A skill task uses a major action unless its rule or the GM specifies otherwise.

### Movement

A major move covers 20 feet and a minor move covers 10 feet before modifiers. Movement can be divided before and after another action on your turn. Difficult terrain costs twice the usual movement. You cannot finish movement in another creature's space. Ordinary melee reach is 5 feet; a spear has 10-foot reach. Slowed cannot reduce movement below 0 feet.

### Dodge

Spend a major action. Direct attacks against you are Diminished until the start of your next turn. Dodge does not modify saving-throw attacks.

### Hinder

Spend a major action to distract or obstruct one creature within 30 feet using a plausible method it can perceive. No check is required. Its next attack or skill check on its next turn is Diminished; the effect then ends, or expires at the end of that turn. Hinder does not affect saving throws. Repeated uses do not stack on one target.

### Attacking

For a **direct attack**, roll the appropriate attack skill. A sword uses Melee, a bow uses Ranged, and a punch uses Unarmed. On a hit, resolve damage. For a **save attack**, the target makes the listed saving throw; do not also roll to hit. An **area attack** requires a separate save from each affected creature, including allies unless the effect says otherwise. Apply any listed lesser effect on a successful save; otherwise success negates the effect.

### Multiple Attacks

Your first ordinary attack uses a major action. An additional attack requires a minor action and permission from another weapon or a feature. You can use a second wielded weapon for one additional attack; simply changing weapons does not grant an extra attack. Each permission grants one additional attack, unless stated otherwise. Extra-attack permissions never grant extra actions.

Your second attack between the start of one turn and the start of your next turn is Diminished. Your third and later attacks in that interval have Greater Diminishment. Count Response attacks and offensive spell castings; an area spell counts as one attack, not one per target. For a save attack, apply the corresponding Enhancement to the target's save instead. These modifiers join the normal ±2 Enhancement/Diminishment limit.

**Example:** A warrior attacks with a sword using a major action, then attacks with a dagger using a minor action at Diminishment. Their final minor action can be used to move or reserved for a legal Response. The dagger does not give them a free action or a third attack.

### Responses

You can reserve up to two unused minor actions at the end of your turn. Declare a trigger for each and the Response feature or tactic you will use. A trigger must be one that the feature allows. Before your first turn, you have no saved actions. Saved actions expire at the start of your next turn.

When a trigger occurs, spend one saved minor action and pay the Response's resource cost. You cannot respond while unable to act. A Response attack must be within reach or range and counts toward multiple-attack penalties. There are no automatic opportunity attacks and no generic permission to attack merely because an enemy moves. Counter Attack and Shadow Kick occur after the triggering attack resolves; Disrupting Blow interrupts casting and Pin Down interrupts movement, as their entries specify.

### Combat Modifiers


| Circumstance                           | Modifier             |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Cover                                  | Diminished           |
| Heavy cover                            | Greater Diminishment |
| Dim light                              | Diminished           |
| Darkness or blindness                  | Greater Diminishment |
| Ranged attack at melee range           | Diminished           |
| Ranged attack into melee               | Diminished           |
| Long range                             | Diminished           |
| Unarmed attack against an armed target | Diminished           |


 

Apply visibility penalties only when the action requires vision. Total cover prevents direct targeting through the obstruction. Nightvision removes the dim-light penalty, not darkness or blindness.

Long range extends beyond a weapon's listed range to twice that range, which is its maximum. If a ranged attack into melee misses, roll percentile dice: on 01–10 it instead hits one random other creature adjacent to the intended target, if any. Resolve an ordinary hit against that creature; it cannot critically hit. Accuracy removes this risk.

### Hiding

You can attempt Stealth as a major action when cover, darkness, or distraction could plausibly conceal you. On success, you are Unseen by observers from whom you could hide. You are Unknown to a creature only if it also has no reason to suspect your presence. The GM determines this from the scene; the same roll establishes the degree of concealment. Obvious exposure ends concealment. Attacking ends either condition after that attack resolves. A creature may use a major action and an Awareness check to locate a hidden creature when circumstances permit; this is not an opposed roll.

### Duration Conventions

An effect lasting 1 round ends at the start of its user's next turn. An effect lasting 3 rounds ends at the start of that user's third subsequent turn. A condition's successful save can end it sooner. Reapplying the same effect refreshes its duration but does not multiply its strength. Features that activate on a hit cost no separate action unless specified; tactics always pay their stated minor action.

## 7 Damage, Defeat, and Recovery



### Wounds and Suffering

**Wounds are remaining health.** Track current Wounds separately from maximum Wounds. Damage removes current Wounds; healing restores them, never above the maximum. A standard hit deals 1 wound, Heavy damage deals 2, and Extreme damage deals 1d6. Wounds cannot fall below 0

Suffering represents pain, shock, and disruption. You can have at most two levels. Each level imposes −1 tier on all skill checks, attacks, and saving throws, including Death Saves. These penalties stack with Broken and apply outside the ±2 circumstance limit. They do not affect flat percentage saves such as Armor Saves or initiative rolls.

Spend a minor action and 1 Stamina to remove one level of Suffering.

### Resolve Each Hit in This Order

1. **Calculate damage.** Include Heavy attacks, critical damage, and any declared bonuses. For a Lesser Attack, roll 01–33 to permit wound damage; on 34–100 its wound damage is 0 regardless of critical or other wound bonuses. It still counts as a hit.
2. **Roll protective saves.** Apply Armor, then any eligible Brace and Ethereal saves. Each successful save reduces damage by 2, to a minimum of 0 Roll each at most once per hit.
3. **Make one Endurance check.** Use this check for Suffering and applicable Uncanny Resistance or Weakness. On success, Resistance prevents up to 1 remaining wound and prevents Suffering. On failure, Weakness adds 1 wound after protective saves, and the hit causes one level of Suffering (or the amount specified by its source).
4. **Apply wound loss and Suffering.** Use the target's state before this hit when making the check. If the target reaches 0 Wounds, resolve Defeat. If it was already at 0 and would take at least 1 wound after defenses, resolve a Death Save.
5. **Apply other on-hit effects.** An effect requiring a wound triggers only if the final damage is at least 1 An effect requiring Suffering triggers only if the target actually gained a level, not when already at the maximum.

A hit reduced to 0 wounds still requires the Endurance check and can cause Suffering. Zero final wound damage never triggers a Death Save. Make one Suffering check per hit, not per wound. A Death Save is a separate check. A spell attack negated by its target's successful save is not a hit and causes no Suffering.

### Armor, Resistance, and Damage Types

An Armor Save is a flat percentile roll, not a skill check. Light armor saves on 01–15, medium on 01–20, and heavy on 01–30. A shield makes direct melee attacks against you Diminished while you wield it. Different armor bonuses add percentage points, to a maximum save of 95%. Natural Armor applies only without worn armor.

Armor Piercing caps the final Armor Save at 10%, after bonuses, unless Rune Armor protects it. Armor protects against external damaging hits of any type unless an effect ignores it. Bleeding, Burning, Poisoned damage, Death Curse damage, and voluntary self-damage bypass Armor, Brace, and Ethereal; applicable Resistance or Weakness still works.

The damage types are **Cold, Eldritch, Enchanted, Fire, Light, Physical, Runic, Storm, and Toxin**. A hit has one type unless expressly stated. If Resistance and Weakness apply to the same type, they cancel. Multiple instances of either do not stack.

Before making a weapon attack, you may spend 1 Sorcerous Power to change its damage type to Enchanted. This uses no additional action. If multiple features change an attack's type, choose one; do not apply several types to the same damage.

### Lesser and Improvised Attacks

Unarmed attacks normally use the Lesser Attack rule. Bite/Claws, Horns, Brawler, and Elder Oak remove that wound penalty for the attacks they cover. An unarmed attack against an armed creature is still Diminished unless a rule expressly removes that penalty.

Improvised weapons use the relevant attribute without attack skill ranks and make Lesser Attacks. Rowdy removes both restrictions. The GM determines whether an object can reasonably be used one-handed, two-handed, or thrown; being large does not automatically give it Great Weapon.

### Falling

A fall of 10 feet or less normally deals no wounds. For each full 10 feet beyond that, take 1 Physical damage, to a maximum of 6 wounds, as one damage event that bypasses Armor, Brace, and Ethereal. Make a Reflexes save before damage; success reduces the total by 1 wound. A damaging fall also knocks you Prone. Apply these rules when Levitation or Flight ends without sufficient safe descent.

### Defeat, Awakening, and Death

1. **Defeat:** When damage reduces you to 0 Wounds, fall Prone and Unconscious. This initial reduction does not itself require a Death Save. Roll 1d3 hours for your unaided awakening time.
2. **Further damage:** Each subsequent damaging hit at 0 Wounds requires a Death Save: an Endurance check. Failure means death. Success leaves you alive at 0 Wounds. This damage does not reset an existing awakening timer.
3. **Aid:** An ally can spend 1 uninterrupted minute and succeed on Medicine to awaken you at 0 Wounds. A failed attempt may be retried, each attempt taking another minute. Appropriate magic can also awaken you.
4. **Unaided awakening:** When the timer expires, make a Death Save. Success awakens you at 0 Wounds; failure means death.
5. **Fragile:** If you awaken at 0 Wounds, you are Fragile but can act. Further positive damage knocks you Unconscious and requires a Death Save. If you survive, begin a new 1d3-hour awakening timer.
6. **Healing:** Regaining at least 1 Wound ends Fragile and awakens you if Unconscious from Defeat. Healing does not revive the dead.
7. **Broken:** Whenever you awaken from Defeat, you are Broken. All skill checks, attacks, and saves suffer −1 tier, stacking with Suffering. Broken does not stack with itself. Healing Wounds does not remove it; Long Recovery does.



### Rest and Medicine


| Recovery      | Duration   | Benefits                                                                                                  |
| ------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Minor Rest    | 10 minutes | Heal 1 Wound; remove 1 Suffering; restore 1 Sorcerous Power and 1 Stamina. Maximum three per 24 hours.    |
| Full Rest     | 8 hours    | Restore Wounds, Sorcerous Power, Stamina, and Luck to their maxima; remove all Suffering. Broken remains. |
| Long Recovery | 3 days     | Remove Broken and gain the benefits of a Full Rest.                                                       |


 

You can benefit from at most three Minor Rests in a rolling 24 hours, or five with Peaceful. A rest requires safety and relative inactivity; fighting or strenuous travel interrupts it. Full Rest benefits can be gained once per 24 hours. Long Recovery requires three consecutive days of shelter, adequate food and water, and no strenuous adventuring.

A conscious character can rest while Fragile; an Unconscious character cannot begin a Minor Rest without first awakening. During a Minor Rest, each target may receive one Medicine check for an additional healed Wound. Multiple healers do not grant repeated attempts on the same target during that rest. A healer may treat themself or one other target per rest. Failed treatment grants no additional healing but does not remove the normal rest benefits.

## 8 Conditions

Conditions apply as soon as they are inflicted. If a condition permits a save, make it at the start of each of your turns. Success removes it unless its entry instead reduces it to a lesser condition. On failure, its ongoing restrictions continue and any periodic damage or forced behavior occurs. Do not also apply periodic damage when the condition is first inflicted unless the source says to do so.

An effect's stated duration is its maximum duration. If no duration is given, a condition with a save lasts until that save succeeds or its source is removed. Held and Immobile also end when the restraint is removed; Controlled ends if the controller dies or the controlling effect ends. Save separately for different conditions, but do not stack multiple copies of one condition. Use only the stronger of Fear/Horror, Dazed/Staggered, and Vulnerable/Greater Vulnerable. Reducing one of these conditions does not grant another save that same turn.

Outside combat, make ordinary turn-based condition saves and apply their periodic effects every 6 seconds while timing matters. Dawn and hourly saves keep their stated schedules. Heavy armor's Slowed and Loud persist while worn and permit no condition save.

### Bleeding

At the start of your turn, make an Endurance save. On failure, take 1 Physical damage. This does not cause Suffering, but still calls for the same Endurance result when checking relevant Resistance or Weakness. It can trigger a Death Save.

### Blinded

Vision-dependent checks and attacks have Greater Diminishment. Make a Faith save to end supernatural temporary blindness. Physical darkness uses visibility rules rather than this condition.

### Blinded (Permanent)

As Blinded, but no condition save is allowed. Magic explicitly described as restorative can remove it.

### Broken

All checks, attacks, and saves suffer −1 tier. Only Long Recovery removes it. See Defeat.

### Burning

Make an Endurance save. On failure, take 1 Fire damage, then make the usual damage Endurance check for Suffering and Resistance or Weakness. A major action spent extinguishing flames with water or smothering them ends ordinary Burning.

### Chilled or Slowed

Reduce each movement allowance by 5 feet, to a minimum of 0 Make an Endurance save to end it unless caused by worn equipment or another persistent physical source. Chilled and Slowed do not add their movement penalties together.

### Confused

Make a Willpower save. On failure, use available movement to approach the nearest ally by a reasonably safe route, then make one normal attack against that ally if possible. If no ally can be reached or attacked, take no hostile action that turn. The GM resolves ties randomly.

### Controlled

Make a Willpower save. On failure, your controller directs your normal actions this turn. Control grants no extra actions and cannot force you to spend Luck. Orders to inflict direct self-harm allow an immediate additional Willpower save before you obey.

### Cursed (Minor)

Your skill checks, attacks, and saves are Diminished. Make a Faith save at dawn to end the curse.

### Cursed (Greater)

Your skill checks, attacks, and saves have Greater Diminishment. Make a Faith save at dawn to end the curse.

### Cursed (Death)

As Greater Curse. Also make a Faith save each hour; on failure, take 1 Eldritch damage, causing Suffering normally. An hourly success prevents that damage but does not end the curse; a successful dawn save ends it.

### Dazed

You have one fewer minor action on your turn. Make a Willpower save to end it.

### Fear

Your attacks and non-save skill checks are Diminished, and you cannot willingly approach the source of fear. Make a Willpower save to end it.

### Fortified

Direct attacks against you are Diminished. Lasts for the source effect’s duration; if none is given, until the start of the grantor’s next turn.

### Fragile

You are awake at 0 Wounds. Further positive damage causes Unconsciousness and a Death Save. Healing at least 1 Wound ends this condition.

### Frozen

You cannot move or take actions. Make an Endurance save to end it.

### Held

You cannot move away from the creature or object holding you. You can otherwise act. Make an Athletics or Reflexes save, your choice, to escape. The holder must maintain physical contact and can release you without an action.

### Horror

Your attacks and non-save skill checks have Greater Diminishment. Use your available movement to flee the source by the safest available route. A successful Willpower save reduces Horror to Fear.

### Immobile

You cannot move from your position, but can otherwise act. Make an Athletics save to break free when the restraint can physically be broken.

### Loud

Your Stealth checks are Diminished. Ends when the source of the noise is removed or silenced.

### Poisoned

Make an Endurance save. On failure, take 1 Toxin damage, causing Suffering normally, and become Dazed until the start of your next turn. This poison-derived Dazed ends when Poisoned ends; unrelated Dazed effects follow their own rules.

### Prone

Melee attacks against you are Enhanced; ranged attacks against you are Diminished. Standing costs a minor action. You can crawl at half your normal movement. No save ends Prone.

### Staggered

You have two fewer minor actions on your turn, to a minimum of 0 A successful Willpower save reduces Staggered to Dazed.

### Stealth (Unknown)

Your next attack against a creature unaware of your presence gains Greater Enhancement. That attack reveals you and ends this condition.

### Stealth (Unseen)

Your next attack against a creature that cannot see you gains Enhancement. That attack reveals you and ends this condition.

### Stunned

You cannot take actions or move voluntarily. Make an Endurance save to end it.

### Unconscious

You are unaware of your surroundings and cannot act or move voluntarily. Unconsciousness from Defeat ends under the recovery rules; other sources specify their own duration or awakening method.

### Vexed

Your nonattack skill checks, including saves, are Diminished. Your attacks are unaffected. Make a Willpower save to end it.

### Vulnerable

Direct attacks against you are Enhanced. Make an Endurance save to end it.

### Vulnerable (Greater)

Direct attacks against you gain Greater Enhancement. Make an Endurance save to end it.

### Multiclassing

You can hold levels in up to three classes, within the total character-level limit of 10. Your first level in another class grants its starting role bonuses and level 1 feature. It does not grant another starting skill allocation, background, equipment kit, or specialization.

**Example:** A Warrior 3 who gains Mage 1 becomes a fourth-level character, Warrior 3/Mage 1. They gain the Magus starting bonuses and Ancient Secrets, including its Circle access, spells, and Soul capacity. They do not gain Mage starting skill ranks. Their next Mage level grants the Mage level 2 Spell Technique. Their specialization follows total character level.

## 12 Class and Specialization Features

These features are granted by class or specialization progression. Permanent bonuses from different named features stack unless stated otherwise. *You coax a dangerous tincture from the materials the land provides.*

When your spell or feature restores Wounds to another creature, you may spend 1 Stamina to restore one additional Wound to one target. Use this once per casting or activation, with no extra action.

*The wilderness teaches you both its dangers and its hidden language.*

Gain access to Nature magic, learn up to two Nature spells, and learn one Weapon Technique.

## 14 Tactics

Every tactic costs **1 Stamina and 1 minor action**. Pay the cost once when using it. A Response tactic spends a saved minor action; an on-hit or on-critical tactic spends an unused minor action at that moment, even during a Response. You cannot use it without an available action. Only one on-hit tactic may modify a given hit. Class features with their own on-hit resource costs are separate.

An activation that enhances a later attack does not include that attack's action cost. Shield Bash and Response tactics expressly include their attack in the tactic's minor action. An on-hit tactic does not grant another attack. Unless specified otherwise, a prepared tactic affects your next eligible attack this turn and is lost if unused.

### Armor Breaker

*You prepare a blow aimed at a defensive weakness.*

Before your next weapon attack this turn, activate this tactic. That attack gains Armor Piercing. **Prerequisite:** Deadly.

**Response:** After an enemy within your melee reach resolves an attack against you, make one melee attack against it. You must remain able to act. **Prerequisite:** Combat Training.

When you critically hit, add 1 wound to the attack before defenses. **Prerequisite:** Deadly.

On a hit, force a Reflexes save. On failure, the target’s next attack before the end of its next turn is Diminished. **Prerequisite:** Veteran.

On a melee hit, force an Athletics save. On failure, the target falls Prone.

## 15 Magic

Magic is divided into orders: traditions connected to particular powers, beings, or ways of understanding the world. A character can study more than one order, but must have access to an order before learning its spells.

### Learning and Requirements

To learn a spell, you need access to its order, Sorcerous Potential at least equal to its level, an empty Sorcerous Soul slot, and a suitable source such as a teacher, tome, or inscription. Study for 8 hours and pass a check using the order’s casting skill. Failure permits another attempt after another 8 hours. Features that explicitly teach spells do so immediately and supply the Soul capacity stated by the core rules. An order’s access alone teaches nothing.

Every casting requires knowledge of the spell, consciousness, the ability to act, and compliance with its targets, materials, and special requirements. Class and specialization restrictions remain in force. A Necromancer’s restricted spells still require night or a place of death; Shadowmage’s restricted spells still require dim light or darkness unless independent access removes that restriction.

**Sorcerous Power** pays for empowerment and other explicit costs. A normal casting costs no Power unless its entry says otherwise. **Sorcerous Potential** limits the highest spell level you can learn, to a maximum of 5. **Sorcerous Soul** is the number of spells you can know; each spell occupies one slot. Forgetting one at the end of a Full Rest frees its slot but does not teach a replacement.

### Actions and Empowerment

Casting normally uses one major action. A longer casting time requires uninterrupted concentration and the ability to act throughout. Fighting, beginning another spell, becoming unable to act, or moving away from required materials interrupts the casting and requires a new attempt. Ordinary brief speech does not interrupt a ritual. Pay additional costs and declare empowerment when casting begins; spent resources are not refunded if interrupted.

Spend **1 Sorcerous Power** to cast a spell **Empowered**, applying the entry’s Empowered effect in addition to, or instead of, its base effect as expressly stated. You may empower a spell only once. Free empowerment and resource substitution work as their granting features describe. An additional stated cost is separate from the 1 Power for empowerment.

A weapon-conjuring spell creates a weapon, not an attack. Attack using your normal weapon action and its listed skill afterward. Conjured weapons do not grant technique training, additional attacks, or extra hands unless stated; they cannot be sold or stockpiled after their spell ends. A beneficial casting requires no success roll unless an entry specifies one.

### Spell Techniques

Each time you gain a spell technique, choose **Potent, Far-Reaching, Lasting, or Subtle**. A learned technique applies to any spell that meets its conditions; you do not learn it separately for each spell.

You can apply **only one technique per casting**, with **no additional Sorcerous Power cost** beyond any empowerment you choose.

Apply at most one learned technique per casting. **Potent** adds 1 wound to one target of an Empowered spell’s immediate damage before defenses. It does not increase healing, recurring damage, summons, retaliation, or later weapon damage. **Far-Reaching** adds 30 feet to numerical casting range, without enlarging areas or modifying self, touch, a later command range, or a travel-distance limit. **Lasting** doubles a Focus spell’s listed duration, without increasing its use limits or Focus slots. **Subtle** removes ordinary casting gestures and speech, but cannot replace sound explicitly required for a song, chant, signal, or heard effect.

### Targets, Touch, and Perception

Perceive the creature, object, or point you target unless the entry expressly permits a known distant creature, remembered location, or prepared anchor. Solid cover blocks ordinary targeting. Areas do not extend through solid barriers unless expressly stated. Touch requires physical contact; all targets of a multiple-touch casting must be within reach and touched during that casting.

A willing creature knowingly accepts an effect. An Unconscious ally may receive healing, protection, or awakening unless the spell requires conscious agreement. Shared thoughts, shared senses, dreams, spiritual projection, and willing travel require conscious agreement. You can include yourself as an ally or willing target when otherwise eligible.

Hostile spirits are disembodied or explicitly spiritual beings acting against the protected creature. Where a spell refers to **unholy creatures**, use undead, hostile spirits, and hostile Forbidden summons; the label does not reveal moral character or detect lies. A creature’s explicit immunity still applies even if a spell’s target category includes it.

### Attack and Damage Resolution

A **direct spell attack** rolls the order’s casting skill with its associated attribute. A successful attack within your critical range adds 1 wound. A spell calling for a save does not also roll to hit. Successful initial saves negate the effect unless a lesser result is stated. Saving-throw attacks cannot critically hit.

Resolve each damaging hit under the normal sequence: calculate damage; roll applicable Armor, Brace, Ethereal, and expressly granted protective saves; make Endurance for Suffering and relevant Resistance or Weakness; apply wound loss and Suffering; resolve Defeat or a Death Save; then apply other on-hit effects. A successful protective save reduces damage by 2, minimum 0. A hit reduced to 0 damage may still cause Suffering. Magical or mental damage does not bypass armor automatically.

**Armor Piercing** caps the final Armor Save at 10% unless Rune Armor protects it. **Uncanny Resistance** prevents 1 remaining wound of its type and prevents Suffering when the damage Endurance check succeeds. **Weakness** adds 1 wound of its type after protective saves when that check fails. Resistance and Weakness of the same type cancel; identical copies do not stack.

Offensive castings count toward multiple-attack penalties. Your second attack between the start of one turn and your next is Diminished; the third and subsequent attacks have Greater Diminishment. For a save attack, apply corresponding Enhancement to each target’s initial save instead. Area spells count as one attack, not one per target. Later condition saves do not inherit the attack-count bonus. Automatic retaliation and secondary damage expressly granted by a spell are part of their triggering effect and supply no extra action, additional-attack permission, critical roll, or chained on-hit tactics.

Voluntary self-damage, condition damage from Bleeding, Burning, or Poisoned, and similar internal damage bypass Armor, Brace, and Ethereal. Resistance and Weakness still apply where relevant. One damage event causes one Suffering check, not one per wound.

### Focus, Durations, and Repeated Effects

Your first learned spell gives Focus 1 if it was 0. If you know spells, Focus becomes at least 2 at character level 4 and at least 3 at level 7. A Focus spell occupies one slot until it ends. Multiple targets or summons expressly included in one casting share that slot. Maintaining Focus takes no action. If you exceed capacity, end an existing spell before the new one takes effect. You may voluntarily end your own spell without an action.

A damaging hit that actually increases your Suffering requires a Willpower save after resolution; failure ends one maintained spell of your choice. Unconsciousness ends all Focus spells. Sustained effects cannot continue after Focus ends; already inflicted damage, completed travel, and explicitly retained ordinary material are not reversed.

One-round effects end at the start of the caster’s next turn; three-round effects end at the start of the caster’s third subsequent turn. An entry specifying the target’s next turn follows that wording. A condition’s successful save can end it sooner. An instant spell’s conditions follow their normal saves without occupying Focus. A Focus spell’s sustained conditions also end when that spell ends.

Identical spells do not stack. Recasting refreshes duration without multiplying strength or duplicating a once-per-duration benefit while an earlier casting remains active. Different named circumstance modifiers combine normally, to a limit of +2 or −2; Suffering and Broken apply afterward. Bright light can suppress a shadow benefit without ending its spell when the entry says so.

Recurring damage or restraints occur at most once per creature per turn when the entry says so, including the initial casting when it also triggers the effect. Repeatedly pushing a creature across a boundary on one turn cannot bypass that limit. Any initial damage and later damage are separate events, but there is no extra periodic damage immediately upon inflicting a condition unless stated.

### Healing and the Restoration Limit

Healing never exceeds maximum Wounds. Restoring at least 1 Wound ends Fragile and awakens a creature Unconscious from Defeat; awakening from Defeat still causes Broken. Ordinary healing does not remove Broken, revive the dead, or restore missing organs unless stated. A creature cannot receive healing incompatible with its living, undead, or construct nature.

**Restoration limit:** A creature may benefit from a particular named spell’s restoration of Wounds, Stamina, or removal of Suffering only **once per rolling 24 hours**, regardless of caster. Where a casting grants several benefits or a sequence of healing, this is one restoration event covering that casting’s entire benefit. Other effects of the spell can still work on later castings unless independently limited. Different named spells have separate limits. Undead healing uses the same rule; reanimating the same remains does not reset it. Draughts, life-draining attacks, sacrifices, and conjured elixirs cannot circumvent this limit.

A spell with a more restrictive limit, such as once in a lifetime, uses its stated limit. An entry that merely grants an extra condition save does not remove Suffering or restore resources unless expressly stated. Suppressing healing does not consume a target’s restoration benefit until it actually receives one.

### Illusions, Knowledge, and Memory

Illusions supply only the senses and space named by their entries. They cannot support weight, deal wound damage, create a real weapon, or compel behavior unless an explicit rule grants that effect. A creature can spend a major action investigating an illusion with Awareness; success identifies the false appearance and lets that observer see through it. Physical contradiction also reveals it. A creature can still choose to act on a believable illusion before investigating.

Information magic reveals only its stated subject and time window. Spirits may be unavailable or ignorant. A sensory impression is truthful about what it shows but may be incomplete. No memory spell automatically predicts the future, supplies secret thoughts, or reveals an entire mystery. A per-subject information limit applies regardless of caster. These procedures resolve uncertainty with one check or save, without opposed rolls.

### Portals, Travel, and Anchors

Teleportation crosses no intervening spaces and does not trigger movement Responses. All destinations must accommodate the traveler and carried equipment safely. If a spell fails because no safe destination exists, paid actions and resources remain spent. A gate permits only the crossings stated; each direction counts as a crossing. Closing a gate cannot sever a creature: a creature partway through is placed harmlessly on the side containing most of its body.

Known or prepared anchors are exact locations, circles, or unique keys specified by the spell. They are not arbitrary destinations invented when casting. An unknown realm requires discovery and a safe anchor before its gate spell can be used. Planar passage is genuine travel, not invisibility or invulnerability; travelers use their ordinary senses, movement, and defenses in the destination realm. Spells that project awareness instead leave the body where stated and cannot deliver attacks or other spells remotely.

### Storage, Echoes, and Reflection

Runestone occupies one Focus slot until released or expired. Its stored instant spell does not require another maintained slot. Record the caster’s final relevant casting tier at creation before later circumstances; activation applies new target defenses, line of sight, and applicable circumstances without copying the original scene’s concealment benefits. The bearer must be able to act and satisfy target and anchor requirements. One stone stores one spell, and a caster may have only one charged Runestone at a time; charging another ends the old one. Breaking it releases nothing. No duplication, storage inside storage, or transfer of techniques is possible.

Arcane Echo records only the specifically eligible spell cast after it. Its later effect uses your current casting ability, spends its own major action, observes target requirements, and cannot record itself or create another echo. Its immediate wound damage, if any, is reduced by 1 to a minimum of 0 before defenses; a zero-damage echo still counts as a hit on a successful attack. Other permitted effects use their ordinary unempowered rules.

A reflected spell receives one new save by its original caster and otherwise uses the original unempowered or Empowered effect as actually cast. It cannot target unrelated creatures, affect an area, duplicate a summon, or reflect again. If the original caster is not a legal perceived target within the ward’s range, no reflection occurs and the ward remains available. The original successful save still has its normal effect, including any stated successful-save damage.

## 17 Companions and Summoned Creatures

A companion is a persistent ally, not disposable equipment. A summoned creature is sustained by a spell and disappears when that spell ends. Neither grants its owner extra actions.

### Actions and Commands

A companion or summon acts immediately after its controller. It has one major action, two minor actions, and one free action, using its own movement. On your turn, spend a minor action to command one creature within 60 feet that can hear or otherwise perceive you. Give a brief instruction for that turn. It can then use its actions to carry it out, including making one attack with its major action. Additional attacks require an explicit feature and use minor actions and normal penalties.

Without a command, a creature can move, Dodge, or protect itself by withdrawing, but cannot attack, use an offensive ability, activate a tactic, or command another creature. If you are Unconscious, it seeks safety or stays near you as the GM considers appropriate. A creature cannot act on the same round twice because control changes. Creating a summon does not include a free command.

You may have one active persistent companion at a time, even if several features grant companion choices. Summons are separate, but each uses a Focus slot and its own command. A creature cannot reserve Responses unless a specific profile or feature grants one.

### Reading Creature Profiles

Listed attack and save tiers are ready-to-use totals. Apply circumstances, Suffering, and Broken normally. Other checks use the profile's Other Checks tier unless a listed aptitude provides a bonus. Creatures have no Luck, Stamina, spellcasting, or techniques unless specified. All start with a critical range of 01–03. Natural attacks deal their listed normal damage rather than Lesser Attack damage and do not take the armed-opponent penalty.

**Small companions:** Raven, owl, eagle, and bat can fly at their normal movement; monkey and snake can climb at their normal movement. Each deals 1 Physical damage with a natural attack. Choose one aptitude—Awareness, Stealth, or Athletics—which receives Enhancement. A bat can navigate ordinary darkness within 20 feet using hearing; deafness prevents this.

**Direwolf:** A sure-footed hunter. Its bite deals 1 Physical damage, and Survival checks to track by scent gain Enhancement.

**Panther:** A patient ambush predator. Its claws deal 1 Physical damage, and Stealth checks gain Enhancement.

**Bear:** A powerful and enduring beast. Its claws deal 1 Physical damage, and Athletics checks gain Enhancement.

**Spider of Kadthor:** An unnatural climber with a 1-Eldritch-damage bite. It climbs walls and ceilings at its normal movement distances. **Elemental:** Its strike deals 1 damage. Choose earth (Physical), air (Storm), fire (Fire), or water (Cold) when summoned. It has Uncanny Resistance to that type. Air elementals fly and water elementals swim at their normal movement; earth and fire elementals move on solid ground. Greater Undead does not affect elementals.

**Grave Servitor:** Its crude strike deals 1 Physical damage. It is undead and immune to Fear and Poisoned, but otherwise suffers damage and Suffering normally. Greater Undead increases its Wounds.

### Injury, Recovery, and Replacement

Persistent companions use the ordinary Defeat, Death Save, Medicine, and rest rules. A summon reduced to 0 Wounds disappears immediately and makes no Death Save. Companion and summon attacks do not inherit their controller's critical bonuses, spell bonuses, or talents unless a feature specifically grants that benefit.

A dead or permanently lost companion can be replaced after three days spent seeking and befriending a suitable creature in an appropriate environment, with the GM's agreement. Replacement costs no XP but requires the opportunity in the story. Companions and summons gain only the improvements expressly granted by features such as Wild Frenzy.
